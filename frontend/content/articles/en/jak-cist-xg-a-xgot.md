## xG: how good the chance was

**xG** (expected goals) is the probability that a given shot ends in a goal. It is calculated from the shot location, the type of situation and other circumstances. A penalty has a high xG, a shot from long range and a tight angle a very low one. The sum of xG over a match says how many goals a team would score on average from such chances.

## xGOT: how well the shot was taken

**xGOT** is calculated only from shots on target and also takes into account where the ball was heading. A shot into the corner has a higher xGOT than a weak effort down the middle. That is why xGOT measures finishing quality and goalkeeping rather than chance quality.

## What the “Goals − xGOT” gap does

- **A positive gap** means a team scores more than its shots on target would suggest. Either the finishing is exceptional, or it was lucky, or opposing goalkeepers made mistakes.
- **A negative gap** is the opposite: the team scores less than we expect.

Short runs swing a lot. After five matches the gap is mostly chance. Only over dozens of matches does it become a characteristic of the team.

## How to read it in the Shooting trend chart

The bar is the actual goals in the match. The dots above or below it are xGOT and xG. A bar below the dots means the team scored less than the chances promised. Switch to “xG breakdown” to see how many expected goals came from open play and how many from set pieces.

## What to watch out for

- xG is not a prediction of the result. It is a description of chance quality.
- Different data providers use different models, so the numbers do not match between websites.
- Own goals and some situations are not counted in the shooting data, so the sum of goals may not match the result exactly.
