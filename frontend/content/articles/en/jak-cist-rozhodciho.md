## “Appointed out of possible”

A referee can take at most one match per round. That is why the number of “possible” matches equals the number of rounds played. A figure of “4 of 9” means the referee was assigned in four of the nine rounds. It shows how often the league trusts them.

## Fouls and cards: home and away separately

In the match list, fouls and yellow cards are split into **home : away**. The total alone says nothing, because every match goes differently. The split is what matters: if a referee regularly gives more fouls against away teams, it may be the home atmosphere, but it may also be that the away teams play roughly.

## Comparison with other referees

The “Against other referees” card compares how many fouls or cards a team gets with this referee and how many with all the others in the league. The green and red deviations show the direction. They do not explain the cause. A referee does not whistle at random, but teams are not assigned to them at random either, so a deviation can simply be about which matches they were given.

## When the sample is small

For one team and one referee there are usually only a few matches. Below three matches we label the comparison a “small sample”. The more seasons you select, the more stable the estimate.

## How to draw a conclusion

1. Look at how many matches the numbers rest on.
2. Compare home and away.
3. Ask whether the deviation makes sense (a referee with many cards for a team that plays hard).
4. Treat it as one signal among many.
